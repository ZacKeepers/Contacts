Waka-Waka

Waka-Waka was the competition robot developed by FIRST Robotics Competition Team 10101. As a lead designer of the robot, I oversaw the CAD modeling of all its subsystems. I also worked to machine its parts and construct Waka-Waka. The robot competed in four events, including the World Championship.

As Waka-Waka is an old project its design history is not shown.

Overview

Waka-Waka is FRC Team 10101 Critical Hit's competition robot, featuring an omnidirectional swerve drive and a maximum fuel storage capacity of 25 to 30 game pieces. It uses PhotonVision for field positioning, contributes toward every ranking point level, and can drive under the trench and over the bump.

Clyde

The belt indexer subsystem uses 5 belts to pull fuel from the intake toward the column. It efficiently manages internal transfer to ensure smooth game element feeding.

Blinky

The three-column launcher uses three motors to launch three pieces of fuel at once and 10 to 15 pieces per second. It provides high-volume scoring capability during match play.

Pac-Maze

The hopper expands as the intake lowers, providing a maximum storage capacity of 25 to 30 pieces of fuel at a time. This design maximizes collection volume while maintaining compact dimensions.

Wowowowo

The reliable omnidirectional swerve drive chassis allows the robot to maneuver quickly and fluidly on the field. It supports agile navigation while maintaining the strength to play defense.

Pac-Man

The 21-inch intake uses silicone grip rollers on an over-the-bumper system to rapidly collect fuel from the ground. It enables high-speed acquisition from nearly any angle.
